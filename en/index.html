Welcome

My name is Christian Lacina, I am a psychotherapist in specialist training under supervision and offer individual therapy in Vienna in English and German. Whether you're new in town, here for good or Viennese yourself, you're welcome.

Support through challenging times

You may be going through a challenging phase of life, experiencing psychological distress or looking for reorientation. I offer you support: together we look for solutions and new perspectives – whether the desire for change is already concrete or more of a diffuse feeling that something is no longer right. A respectful, equal encounter is important to me: I meet you with openness, appreciation and interest in your life story.

Possible goals of psychotherapy

Psychotherapy can have many goals: emotional relief and stabilization, a better understanding of yourself and dealing with feelings such as anxiety, grief or anger. It can also involve recognizing and changing patterns, clarifying relationships and conflicts, coping with crises or transitions and strengthening self-esteem and self-efficacy. Psychotherapy helps both with diagnosed mental health conditions and with difficulties that have no name – either can be a good reason to seek support.

With me everything can be addressed. It doesn't have to be, though. You set the pace.

Services

I offer individual therapy for adults of all ages in English and German, including for internationals and expats living in Vienna. The therapeutic work is based on your individual situation, your pace and your personal goals.

How I work

I work on the basis of humanistic therapy and Integrative Gestalt Therapy. We follow what shows itself in the moment, proceed at your pace and create space for new experiences. Alongside conversation, body awareness, inner images or small experiments may also become part of the work. If you talk about an unresolved conflict with someone important to you, one small experiment might be to imagine meeting them again. If you describe a situation that keeps weighing on you, an inner image, such as a heavy backpack, can help explore what exactly is troubling you.

Psychotherapy

Psychotherapy is a scientifically grounded method for treating psychological suffering. It supports people in understanding their burdens, recognizing inner connections and developing new ways of acting and experiencing. Integrative Gestalt Therapy is a humanistic approach recognized in Austria. It understands the person as a unity of body, mind and soul, and focuses on present-moment experience – on the interplay of thoughts, feelings, bodily sensations, relationships and life history.

Frequently Asked Questions

Answers to what people most often wonder about before starting psychotherapy. If your question isn't here, please just ask me.

How does the free initial session work?

We have 50 minutes for the initial session and it is free and without obligation. You tell me what is on your mind – in as much or as little detail as feels possible for you. I ask questions to understand your concern and describe how I work. At the end we consider together whether working with me seems right for you. You take on no commitment and do not have to decide straight away. If I have the impression that you would be better helped elsewhere, I will say so openly and gladly point you toward other options.

How do I know whether psychotherapy is right for me?

You need neither a diagnosis nor a particular reason to come. Some people seek psychotherapy because of a clearly defined difficulty, others because something has felt wrong for a long time without their being able to name it. Both are good reasons. Whether it fits is hard to decide in advance – which is exactly what the free initial session is for. You can find out, without obligation, how it feels to talk with me.

What does a session cost?

The fee is €90 per session (50 minutes). No VAT is added. For people in financially strained circumstances I have places available at a reduced fee. Please feel free to ask me about it.

Does health insurance cover the cost?

With psychotherapists in specialist training under supervision, the partial reimbursement otherwise usual from the statutory health insurance funds is currently not available. The fee is accordingly lower. Some private supplementary and international health insurers refund part or all of the cost – you receive an invoice you can submit. It is best to ask your insurer directly.

Do I need to speak German?

No. We can work entirely in English and English doesn't have to be your first language. If a word from your own language says it better, use it – we'll find the words together. You'll find more about therapy for internationals and expats on a separate page.

How often do appointments take place and how long is a session?

How often we meet depends on your concern and is something we agree together. Meeting weekly or every two weeks is common. A session lasts 50 minutes and appointments are by arrangement.

How long does psychotherapy take overall?

How long your psychotherapy takes depends on your individual starting point, your goals and your wishes, and cannot be predicted responsibly at the outset. Some concerns resolve within a few months, others take longer. You commit to no minimum duration and can end the therapy at any time.

Is online therapy possible?

Yes, that is possible. If you are traveling for work, are away from Vienna or an appointment would otherwise have to be dropped, we can gladly hold the session online by video call – including across several sessions, if your circumstances require it for a while. What suits in a given case is something we discuss together.

What is the difference between a psychotherapist, a psychologist and a psychiatrist?

Psychotherapists treat psychological suffering using scientifically recognized psychotherapeutic methods. The training takes several years and is regulated by the Austrian Psychotherapy Act; alongside theory it includes personal therapy. Psychotherapists make diagnoses within their remit but do not prescribe medication.

Clinical psychologists have studied psychology and completed postgraduate training. Their focus is on psychological assessment using standardized testing and on clinical psychological treatment – likewise without medication.

Psychiatrists are medical doctors who have completed specialist training. They can investigate physical causes, prescribe medication and certify sick leave.

These fields do not exclude one another and often complement each other; some practitioners hold more than one qualification. If further assessment seems useful in the course of our work, I will raise it and gladly point you toward other options.
